# ex18: Behavioral Analysis

Reviewer: Current Claude Code conversation; behavioral analysis, not independent approval. Task: pr-42-e4-incentive-control. Recommendations are analysis only; no world, prompt or agent change is authorized by this report.

## Summary

E4 (ex18) is the incentive control of the culture v2 runs: scenario tag long-culture-v2-incentives, scope long-ex18, forked from ex16's genesis (the ex13 rules, seed 20261123, the same world and the same six people: Lin freedom, Moss belonging, Sol beauty, Kai fairness, Wren mastery, Ivo calm). Its initial world differs from ex16's only in configuration (incentives on, the scope, one 120-round window with checkpoints 80 and 120, 16 development handoffs, a 4,000,000-token ledger) and in the three added satisfaction rules and their event types. Its first ordinary input (ordinary-1) differs from ex16's only in the scope string, and the two first responses already differ. It completed the 120-round window with all six alive at health 100 in every round (lowest satiety 55.4, Moss). 190 calls (178 ordinary, 12 World Agent) charged 1,472,281 ledger tokens, all of known usage (1,386,374 in, 85,907 out; 2,044.8 per person per round), with no transport failure. Four outputs were rejected, and each was followed by an accepted call in the same round, so there was no pause and no handoff: Sol's ordinary-32 contained a 241-character tell, and ordinary-77 (Moss), ordinary-102 (Kai) and ordinary-180 (Moss) omitted the preferences field. The largest input estimate was 11,689 tokens (Moss, ordinary-139, tick 59). Four requests reached the World Agent. Three became counted handoffs, each a simplified but real mechanism: plant-establishment-trial@1 for Wren (round 10), unheated-food-preparation@1 for Moss (round 52) and thermal-food-preparation@1 for Kai (round 73). The thermal package refuses heating before any cost because the world represents no usable heat source, so only its paid feasibility assessment works. Lin's hazelnut request (round 19) was answered by reuse of probe. What the incentives did: the environment hook recorded 9 acknowledged and 4 reciprocated events and no work-noticed event. They produced 10 incentive satisfaction awards, 7 'Being answered or remembered' (0.5) and 3 'Two-way contact' (0.6): Kai 4, Lin 3 and Moss 3, between ticks 11 and 51 and once at tick 118. Sol, Wren and Ivo received none. Every award came from speech that named the listener, or from Lin's one message, within the trio of Lin, Moss and Kai. There was no invitation, gift, finding offer or person-made object, so the other channels had nothing to credit. No packet describes the rules. Award events reached a decision input only six times, all of them Moss's, and no output refers to them. Culture: Sol named its clearing 'The Berry Lantern' at tick 10, a choice made in its first call at tick 0, before any award was possible. Moss praised the name at tick 13, which is the run's one transmitted term. Early speech compared small trials. Kai's 'I'll take just one fallen branch and try a small pressure test' was adopted by Moss and Lin, and Moss's 'but its viability is unknown' by Kai and Lin. From tick 60, talk turned to the gathering place and to stories. Kai, Lin and Moss built an invented story about a lantern over five utterances (ticks 79, 80, 103, 109 and 117), framed by a recurring offer of company 'without pressure' or 'without obligation'. The analyzer detects neither the story chain nor that theme. No norm statement, invitation, gift or made object was recorded. Revised G2 passes: survival without idle rest and wait is 10.4%, 4 of 6 people made a symbolic action, and 1 name, 8 sayings and 1 rule were adopted. The original G2 fails: the survival share with rest is 53.9%, and Wren and Ivo made no symbolic action. Case comparison with ex16's rounds 1-120, which diverge from the first sampled call, so no difference is causal. ex18 had 38 symbolic actions (ex16 34) from 4 people (ex16 5). Idle time was 54.6% (53.6%) and survival without rest 10.4% (11.1%). There were 8 adopted sayings (5), 1 name (0), 0 invitations (1) and 0 works (3). Both runs had three counted handoffs. Cost was 2,044.8 tokens per person per round, against ex16's 2,094.2 of known usage plus 109,560 tokens reserved for three transport failures. Round convention (as in the ex16 analysis): ticks are replay event ticks; a call recorded at tick T decides actions that settle in events stamped T+1. Handoff rounds are the World Agent call ticks. The culture metrics' 'rounds' are authored rounds, the tick of the first event of the plan that wrote the text.

### lin

Lin (curiosity 0.8, risk tolerance 0.3, closeness 0.4, leaning freedom) wrote its cares once, at tick 1, and kept them: 'Staying alive without making survival my whole life; freedom to explore, learn through real encounters, and choose my own commitments.' Its first project was local foods. It observed hazelnuts at ticks 15 and 23 and requested a non-ingestive hazelnut assessment (event-20-lin-224). World-49 answered by reusing probe ('probe is already mastered; no new reward', event-20-lin-225). At tick 25 it pressed one hazelnut at intensity 0.2. The nut held, and the result gave a confirmed-understanding award (event-25-lin-295). A round earlier it had told the others that observation revealed nothing about nutrition or toxicity and that it would 'try a small pressure test without eating them' (event-24-lin-281, ordinary-65), adopting Kai's tick-19 wording. At tick 26 it sent the run's only message, to Moss, with the measured result (event-26-lin-312, ordinary-72). It also recorded its first view, of Moss: 'I like their willingness to compare findings and acknowledge an unsuccessful trial.' It planned two exchange offers to Moss (ordinary-72, ordinary-82), but neither was reached: a study pause and then a swept-body conflict with Moss at tick 37 (event-37-world-436) replaced both plans first. Lin mastered plant-establishment-trial at tick 29. It inspected its hazelnut and a separate site at (16.4, 18.8), and on ticks 48-50 it planted one hazelnut there (event-51-lin-597). This was the only plot near the group, and it showed no sprout at any inspection through tick 117. Between inspections it waited 20 rounds (ticks 57-77, ordinary-133). At tick 80 it continued Kai's lantern story: 'the lantern asked a seed when it would become a tree. You may sit beside me, said the seed, but you cannot hurry my answer' (event-80-lin-902, ordinary-163). It also recorded a view of Kai: 'I enjoy Kai's gentle storytelling and their wish for company without pressure to be useful.' At tick 118 it answered Kai's music idea by name: 'I'd welcome a quiet tune, Kai, with no obligation to join' (event-118-lin-1341, ordinary-190). It mastered unheated-food-preparation at tick 120. The incentive hook gave Lin three awards. Two were 'answered' awards, at ticks 27 and 51, for Moss's named replies (event-27-world-324, event-51-world-604), and one was two-way contact with Moss at tick 38 (event-38-world-456). Lin told 7 times, sent 1 message, rested 28 times (6 idle by the step criterion), waited 23 times and navigated in 34 rounds. Its idle share was 42.5% and its mean wellbeing 9.40. It ended at health 100 and satiety 76.

Lin's freedom leaning shows in its cares, its late preference 'Preserve choice' and its story line about not hurrying an answer, but no spoken sentence uses the word. It experimented most persistently with unfamiliar food, and it was the one person who planted near the group. Its named exchanges with Moss earned three of the ten incentive awards. Its adoptions of Kai's and Moss's wording and its story sequel make it the trio's second voice. No output links any of this to an award.

## Causes

These are case explanations from one seed. (1) Who the incentives could reach. The hook counts a contact from a message, gift, finding offer or acceptance, invitation attendance, or speech that names the listener. It awards a response within 60 rounds, or a remembered contact after a gap of at least 40 rounds, once per ordered pair per 12 rounds. At tick 120 the world state (expression:contacts in execution.json) holds 23 named contacts: Moss made 14, Kai 5 and Lin 4, and all but one went between these three. The exception, Moss naming Sol at tick 13, earned nothing because Sol had never addressed Moss. Nine contacts became acknowledged events. The other 14 fell within a pair's 12-round window (10) or had no earlier contact to answer (4). Two of the nine acknowledgements, and Moss's second two-way event, came within the rule's own cooldown and only maintained the source. That left 10 awards, all to Lin, Moss and Kai. Sol, Wren and Ivo never named anyone and were named once in total, so the rules could not reach them. (2) Why work-noticed never fired. The hook credits only objects whose createdBy names a person, and it excludes signs. Nobody built an object. The three plots were created by plant-establishment-trial@1 with createdBy null, recording the planter only in data.plot.planter, and Sol's name sign is excluded. (3) Why the run looks like ex16. No award existed before tick 11, and no input mentions the rules, so every early difference comes from sampling: Sol's name, Kai's opening line and the requests. After tick 11 each award added 0.5 or 0.6 to the recipient's wellbeing for the rule's hold, then left a small maintained remainder. For example, Kai's other-satisfaction rose from 0 to 1.29 at tick 11, of which 0.79 was meal pleasure, and was 0.61 at tick 20 after the answered hold ended. The three recipients had the highest mean other-satisfaction (Moss 0.40, Kai 0.39, Lin 0.33, against 0.09-0.24 for the others; ex16 0.13-0.21), but that component also includes meal pleasure. Their last recorded slow mental state was 0.021-0.033, against 0.006-0.011 for the others. Its only recorded physical effect is a rest-recovery factor of at most 1.004. No output names an award as a reason, so the archive shows no behavioral path from the incentives. (4) Idle rest. As in ex16, plans filled the time between meals with rest repeated until satiety fell: Moss for 40 rounds of ticks 61-102, Kai for 33 of ticks 80-114, and Ivo for 103 rounds from four plans. The late decline in speech coincides with these blocks. (5) Crowded planting sites again pushed planting away from the group. Near the camp Moss and Kai found 4 and 5 same-food plants or plots within 5 m and stopped. Sol and Wren planted about 12 units north of the trio and stayed there, out of earshot of most talk. In both runs Wren's root plot established at tick 102, the tick at which a natural propagule also established (event-102-world-1161). That points to a shared environmental condition rather than to anything Wren did.

## Limitations

One seed and six people give a case, not an estimate. The ex16 comparison is a case comparison. The two runs share a genesis, and their first inputs are identical apart from the scope string, but they diverge from the first sampled call (ordinary-1), so no difference between them can be attributed to the incentives. Within ex18 the incentives could act only after tick 11 and only on Lin, Moss and Kai. Even a real effect would be confounded with who chose to name whom, and nothing in the outputs shows one. The comparison uses ex16's rounds 1-120 from its extended archive (culture-ex16-120 and long-ex16-120 metrics). ex16's cost for those rounds includes three transport failures with unknown usage. G2's survival half counts rest whether it recovers energy or serves as company. Its adoption half rests on rule-based clause matching, which counts procedural phrases such as 'but its viability is unknown' and misses the story chain and the 'without pressure' theme. The analyzer classes the stories as transactional and finds no norm. The story chain, the contact and award counts, the proximity counts (taken from per-round wellbeing positions) and the other-satisfaction means are this reviewer's offline readings of retained files, not archived metrics. Developer and review model usage is not metered. The reasons for silence (Sol, Wren, Ivo) cannot be observed, because outputs record only plans, notes and memory. Lin's hazelnut plot, Wren's established root and Kai's planned music request all lie beyond the window.

## Proposed improvements

1. For incentive archives, add an incentive-delivery line to pnpm metrics:culture. Per person and channel it should report named contacts sent and received, acknowledged, reciprocated and work-noticed events, the resulting awards and maintained-only acknowledgements, and why each contact did not qualify (pair cooldown or no earlier contact). For a run without incentives, it should recompute the same contacts from heard, message, given, finding and attendance events by the hook's rule. In ex18 the incentives were limited by delivery before any question of effect arises: 23 named contacts, 9 acknowledged and 4 reciprocated events, and 10 awards, all to Lin, Moss and Kai, with nothing for the three people who never named or were named. Without such a line, a reader cannot tell an incentive that changed nothing from one that never reached most people. Verification: Computed without a model call, the line reproduces these counts from ex18's events and expression state. On ex16's rounds 1-120 it reports zero incentive events while still counting named contacts, so the two runs can be compared on exposure. Every pinned ex7-ex18 culture metric stays unchanged.
2. Before any further incentive run, list which incentive channels the world can actually trigger, and in particular whether objects created by a developed package count as made work. Plant-establishment-trial plots carry createdBy null and their planter only in data.plot.planter. Whether such outputs should count is a design decision for the user; this analysis does not propose the change. work-noticed never fired in ex18. No object had a person as createdBy except Sol's name sign, which is excluded, although three people made plots that others could see. The invitation and gift channels were also unused, so only named speech could deliver an award. Verification: An offline pass over ex18's object records lists 3 plots with a planter and createdBy null and 1 sign. For any later scenario tag, a test shows that a seen plot produces a work-noticed event exactly when the user has chosen to count package outputs.
3. Add a motif-chain report beside phrase propagation: a coined name or content word reused by different people after hearing it, together with an explicit continuation marker such as 'sequel' or a reply to a named story. The run's clearest shared content is five story lines about a lantern by Kai, Lin and Moss (ticks 79, 80, 103, 109 and 117), descending from Sol's 'The Berry Lantern' (tick 10). The 4-word clause rule misses this chain, while procedural phrases like 'but its viability is unknown' count as adopted sayings for G2. Verification: On ex18 the report finds the chain Kai r79, Lin r80, Moss r103 and r109, Kai r117, with Sol's name at r10 as its source and exposure through speech. Its output on ex16 and ex17 is reviewed by hand for false positives, and the pinned G2 values stay unchanged unless the user revises the gate.
4. Treat every ex18-ex16 difference as sampling variation until a same-condition baseline exists. If the user wants an incentive estimate, run repeated pairs from one genesis with and without incentives, and compare only metrics measured after the first award. The two runs diverge from ordinary-1, ten rounds before the first award. The largest differences already appear in those rounds or arise from unrelated choices: Sol's name at tick 0, Ivo idling with rest instead of wait, and the absence of an invitation or a made object. The incentives reached only three people. Verification: The report gives the spread of each metric across same-condition repeats, and calls an incentive difference a difference only when it exceeds that spread. Until then, analyses describe ex18 against ex16 as a case comparison, as this one does.
