## Causes

Observed demand-to-outcome chains are strong for implementation and execution, and stop before sustained benefit. Sol asked for actual attentive observation; Moss asked for an owned-material arrangement. Both requests explicitly avoided assuming reward, and both World Agent specifications explicitly registered no mental satisfaction. Subsequent contexts accurately reported an empty satisfaction catalogue. Thus mental happiness mechanically remained zero even after successful work; this is a rule-level explanation supported by saved specifications and events. Why no actor later requested an event-grounded satisfaction source is uncertain. Possible contributors include safety-oriented wording, the low-friction food/rest routine, sparse lasting feedback from the two new activities and model planning habits. None is isolated causally here. Continuous geometry and cost are validated and present in real fractional trajectories, but there is no matched grid control. Survival was already feasible under the authored food system and cannot be credited to the new craft or observation.
